Real Estate Websites and CRM Workflows

Real-estate teams need accurate property information and accountable lead follow-up. A website, CRM and operations portal should share enough context to support a buyer without creating conflicting versions of availability or sales status.

Discuss your projectExplore selected work

Property discovery and enquiry

Define the fields that matter to the offered inventory: location, property type, availability and approved supporting documents. Publish only authorized pricing, claims and imagery. A lead enquiry should retain the property context so a sales representative can respond meaningfully. Avoid misleading urgency or implying an investment return that has not been substantiated.

Lead ownership and activity

Map assignment, acceptance, follow-up, visit scheduling and closure reasons. Prevent duplicate leads from silently creating competing owners. Managers need queue health and overdue actions; representatives need relevant next steps. An approved messaging integration should distinguish an attempted message from confirmed delivery and should follow the organization’s contact permissions.

Documents and approvals

Property records can involve sensitive documents and internal notes. Define which information is public, customer-visible or restricted to staff. Keep document access tied to authorization and review changes to published inventory. Integrations should preserve the source identifier so records can be reconciled after imports or failed updates.

Evidence and scoping

The portfolio shows Aloha Estate interface work; use it as an example of a workflow discussion, not proof of financial outcomes or a legally approved transaction system. Bring anonymized lead stages, inventory fields and role definitions. Acceptance should cover assignment conflicts, withdrawn listings, access revocation and restored records. Regulatory content and commercial claims require approval from the responsible business.
