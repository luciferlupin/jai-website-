Retail and D2C Website Solutions

Retail and D2C businesses need a consistent path from product discovery to purchase and post-purchase support. A distinctive storefront helps only when catalog, pricing, availability and checkout remain dependable.

Discuss your projectExplore selected work

Catalog and customer journey

Start with product taxonomy, variants, imagery and the questions buyers ask before purchase. Watches may need readable dimensions and material information; fashion may need size guidance and clear variant availability. Use only specifications supplied by the merchant. Plan collection pages, search, product detail, cart and support as a connected journey rather than independent visual designs.

Stock, payments and operations

Identify the source of truth for price, stock and order status. Provider availability depends on the merchant account and intended markets. Test out-of-stock states, failed payments and canceled orders through approved platform flows. Customer support should see enough order context to help without exposing payment secrets or unnecessary personal data. Delivery promises must come from the operating business.

Performance and measurement

Product media often dominates transfer size. Generate appropriate image sizes, reserve layout space and keep the critical product image available early. Measure catalog-to-cart and checkout handoffs after the required consent. Do not call a checkout click a sale. Reconcile purchases with authoritative order records when an approved analytics integration supports that measurement.

Relevant work and project brief

The portfolio and HANBORO storefront case study offer visual examples for a retail discussion. They do not establish a sales uplift or a universal platform configuration. Bring representative products, the current catalog system, target markets and critical apps. Scope the migration, redirects, integration ownership and launch tests before committing to the storefront architecture.
