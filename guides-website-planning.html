Business Website Planning Guide

A website plan should connect the customer’s questions with the business’s actual offer. The strongest starting point is a clear audience, a small set of useful pages and a dependable enquiry route. Design then helps people understand and navigate that information.

Define audience and offer

Describe the customer, the problem they want solved and the next action they can take. Separate distinct services only when visitors need different information to evaluate them. Use actual portfolio work and approved company facts. Avoid invented reviews, unsupported numbers or a city list that implies offices the business does not operate.

Create the content map

List the questions each page answers and its relationship to other pages. A service page can link to relevant work, scope guidance and contact information. A cost guide should explain variables and refer to approved pricing rather than manufacture a universal quote. Keep valuable existing URLs and record any necessary replacements.

Specify the enquiry journey

Decide whether contact is by form, phone, email or an external messaging service. Explain what happens after a visitor acts. If a form opens WhatsApp, the visitor still needs to send the message; do not report that handoff as a delivered lead. Preserve draft input if the handoff fails and avoid sending personal details into analytics.

Prepare launch ownership

Name owners for content approval, the domain, hosting, repositories and third-party accounts. Test the production domain on phone and desktop, including navigation, errors and the enquiry path. Agree update and support responsibilities before launch. Bring the current website, brand assets and prioritized page list to a discovery discussion so the scope reflects real work.
